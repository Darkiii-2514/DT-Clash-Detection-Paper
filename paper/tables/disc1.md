Across the two buildings, geometry-only triage would prioritise 498 of 1,025 hard clashes (49 %) and category rules 350 (34 %), whereas the proposed method prioritises 27 (2.6 %). The disagreements run in both directions: most clashes that geometry flags are routine sleeved penetrations of slabs and walls, while several that it ranks Minor remove capacity where it matters, such as a duct clipping a girder flange or a mechanical unit cutting a joist chord. Each class can be traced to the governing action, the capacity removed, the demand at that location and the load that depends on the member, which gives an engineer something to check rather than a score to trust. These quantities could also serve as structural features for relevance classifiers learned from past coordination decisions [@hu2019; @lin2019; @koo2026].

The held-out case carries a broader lesson: rules that look neutral can embed assumptions about the structural system. A "typical bay" measured by beams presumes beam-and-slab construction, and "a beam end at a column bears on the column" presumes steel or concrete framing rather than timber joinery. Both held in the development case and failed silently in the held-out one, so automated structural reasoning on BIM data should be tested across structural systems, with reference quantities defined by structural function rather than by element type.

For practice, updates in tens of milliseconds for MEP edits and a few seconds for structural edits allow clashes to be checked *while* modelling rather than in periodic coordination rounds, moving coordination toward the closed control loop described for construction digital twins [@sacks2020].

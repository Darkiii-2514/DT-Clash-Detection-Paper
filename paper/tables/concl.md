This study presented a digital twin-enabled framework that detects structural clashes in federated Revit models and grades them by what they do to the structure, evaluated on two Revit 2027 sample buildings of different structural systems. The main findings are:

1. Exact-mesh clash detection from Revit API data agreed closely with Revit's own solid-intersection filter (F1 = 0.994 and 0.978) once full containment and multi-solid families are handled, and with an R-tree it scaled almost linearly to 82,000 elements.
2. A gravity load-path graph can be built automatically from BIM geometry, Revit joins and member ends, and it conserves load exactly. It also flags members without a modelled support (1.5 % and 8.3 %).
3. Load path-aware grading prioritised 27 of 1,025 hard clashes (2.6 %), against 49 % for geometry-only triage, and the two disagreed in both directions, so penetration depth is a poor proxy for structural severity.
4. The classes were robust to loads and tolerances in the development case but sensitive to the impact thresholds, and in the held-out case also to the consequence thresholds and the contact tolerance, which therefore need calibration against expert judgement.
5. The held-out building exposed rules that assumed a structural system; redefining them by structural function corrected them and changed three of 687 classes in the development case.
6. As a digital twin, the analysis updated in tens of milliseconds for MEP changes and a few seconds for structural changes, with results identical to full recomputation, and wrote its assessment back into Revit.

The most important next step is to validate the severity classes against blinded expert ratings, for which an instrument and analysis scripts are released with the code. Other direct extensions are finite-element verification of the top-ranked clashes, lateral load paths, physical-state data (scans, sensors, construction progress) through the same change interface, and proposing the cheapest reroute that removes a Critical clash.

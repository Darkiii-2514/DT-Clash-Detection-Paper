Clash detection reports thousands of interferences in building information models but cannot tell which ones matter structurally. This paper presents a digital twin-enabled framework that extracts structural and MEP elements through the Revit API with Python, detects clashes on exact meshes, builds a gravity load-path graph that conserves load exactly, and grades each clash by combining a code-informed local capacity-loss index with the load that depends on the member. Revit change events update the analysis incrementally, and results are written back. On two federated models, detection agreed with Revit's solid-intersection filter (F1 0.994 and 0.978). Geometry-only triage prioritised 49 % of 1,025 hard clashes; the proposed method prioritised 2.6 %, including flange and chord cuts that geometry rated minor. A held-out building exposed, and led to correcting, structural-system assumptions. Incremental updates took 37–62 ms for MEP and 2.5–5.6 s for structural changes, identical to full recomputation.

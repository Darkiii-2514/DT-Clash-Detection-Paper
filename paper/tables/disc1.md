Across the two buildings, geometry-only triage would ask coordinators to prioritise 498 of 1,025 hard clashes (49 %) and category rules 350 (34 %). The proposed method prioritises 27 (2.6 %). The reduction is not a matter of setting thresholds more leniently. The disagreements run in both directions: most clashes that geometry flags are routine sleeved penetrations of slabs and walls, whereas several clashes it ranks Minor remove capacity where it matters, such as a duct clipping a girder flange or a mechanical unit cutting a joist chord. Each class is also explainable in engineering terms. It traces back to the governing action, the fraction of capacity removed, the demand at that location, the load that depends on the member, the load without an alternative path, and code-based flags such as "within 4h of column" or "opening > h/3". That gives an engineer something to check rather than a score to trust. This complements relevance classifiers learned from past coordination decisions [@hu2019; @lin2019; @koo2026]: the local impact and consequence computed here could serve as structural features for such models, which at present see only element attributes.

The held-out case carries a broader lesson. Rules that look neutral can embed assumptions about the structural system: a "typical bay" measured by beams presumes a beam-and-slab building, and "a beam end at a column bears on the column" presumes steel or concrete framing rather than timber joinery with tenons. Both assumptions held in the development case and failed silently in the held-out one, and the failure surfaced only because the consequence distribution looked implausible. Automated structural reasoning on BIM data should therefore be tested across structural systems, not only across buildings, and its reference quantities should be defined by structural function (the load a member receives from the floor) rather than by element type.

For practice, incremental updates in tens of milliseconds for MEP edits and a few seconds for structural edits make it feasible to check clashes *while* modelling rather than in periodic coordination rounds. Together with the write-back of severity into the authoring view, this moves coordination towards the closed control loop that Sacks et al. describe for construction digital twins [@sacks2020]. A by-product is a model-quality check: members without a load path (1.5 % and 8.3 % of the structural members here) point to missing supports or modelling conventions that would also trouble downstream structural analysis.
